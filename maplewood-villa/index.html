Maplewood Villa

The Maplewood, redrawn as a transitional-contemporary villa. It keeps the 50-ft lot and the two-car garage, and gains a calmer plan: one line of sight from the front door to the garden, fewer and larger rooms, no angled walls, and a façade of limestone, lime stucco, oak and black steel.

Six ideas behind the plan

Each one fixes something specific in the original layout.

One axis

The front door, the gallery and the great room's west French door sit on one line. You see the garden from the doorstep.

Fewer, larger rooms

Living, family, breakfast and dining collapse into a 20-ft great room and a kitchen that seats ten. Each room keeps its own centreline.

No angled walls

The original's 45° walls are gone. Every wall is orthogonal, so furniture, millwork and ceilings line up.

Tall, low, tall

A 20-ft foyer, a 10-ft gallery, then the 20-ft great room. The lower ceiling between them makes both tall rooms feel taller.

A service spine

Garage, mudroom, scullery, kitchen. Groceries and boots never cross the formal rooms.

Honest materials

Honed limestone, white lime stucco, rift oak, black windows and standing-seam metal. Five materials, used the same way on every face.
