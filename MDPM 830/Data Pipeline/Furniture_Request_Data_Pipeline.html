Step Two · Data Pipeline Model 20%

Responsibility, preparation and constraints

Entering data is not the same as being accountable for it. Creators answer for what they state. The stage owner answers for the rules that make the data trusted. The data owner answers for the request record from start to finish.

What clean and trusted means here

Keep and Shape. Proposed rules, to confirm with the business.
  1. One Request ID, with every material update linked to it
  2. One status from an agreed list, with a defined meaning
  3. A named owner at every stage
  4. Required fields and documents complete before review
  5. Dates in one format and in true order
  6. Duplicate requests checked on an agreed match key

A request that fails the checks goes back to the CMAC and is checked again. The prototype shows why the rules matter: one request appears as both "Awaiting initial review" and "Reviewing", and some activity is dated before submission. We state what preparation must achieve, not how it is engineered.

Constraints

Quality
Floor plans may not be current; submission materials may be missing; status meanings and completion criteria are not yet confirmed; no agreed match key for duplicates.
Frequency
Requests are created by people, intermittently, one at a time. Updates are event-driven, at each decision or status change.
Format
Needs arrive by email, Teams and phone. Attachments include floor plans and PDFs. The prototype limits uploads to 10MB per file and 5MB per reply.
Ownership
Keep and Shape owner: Open (Proposed: Furniture Request Coordinator). Data owner for the request record: Open.
Access
Who may view, update or share each item is to be confirmed. Proposed: a CMAC sees only their own requests.
Retention
Requirements to confirm. Historical WORTS migration is an open decision.

What goes into the PRD and Milestone 3

  • P1 to P3: define what CMACs and coordinators see, and how updates, missing information and changes of responsibility are shown.
  • M1: define how DVO 1, 2 and 6 are measured, what evidence is needed and what counts as success.
  • B1: define the operational questions management needs answered and what the reports must provide.
  • Milestone 3: decide how current and historical data is stored, maintained, kept accurate and made available to the right users.

Still to confirm: coordinator hand-offs, mandatory fields, status definitions, user permissions, measurement baselines and management reporting needs.

Back into this pipeline

Validation patterns improve the form and checklist. Cycle-time history sets realistic service levels. Assignment patterns can suggest the right Furniture Coordinator (Proposed).

Other teams and pipelines

Potential uses: Finance for invoice matching, Procurement for vendor scorecards, Facilities for an asset register (a conditional future opportunity), and HR for workload evidence.

Decisions elsewhere

Volume, price and service-level trends inform leadership decisions on vendors, budget, purchasing policy and staffing.

How the value builds over time

1. A request is handledEach decision, return and update is dated and attributed.
2. Recorded against the Request IDOne request gives a dated timeline.
3. Patterns appear across requestsMany requests show which items are missed and where requests wait. Comparable periods give the baselines that DVO 1, 2 and 6 need.
4. The service improvesBetter checklists, realistic service levels and clearer views produce more complete requests.

More complete requests make the next round of data more reliable, so each cycle starts from better information.

Who collects it, or whether anyone does: vendor lead times, prices and deliveries are already recorded as events, but no one aggregates them. Stage durations have no monitoring yet. Delivery data does not reach an asset register; that register is a conditional future opportunity outside the current scope.

To confirm with the client

  • Which sources are authoritative for building, location and floor-plan information
  • Who is accountable for data quality and the request record throughout its lifecycle

  • Which material updates must be retained, and for how long
  • What baseline and collection method can support claims about reduced follow-up effort
  • Which management decisions operational reporting must support
